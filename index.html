Biliteral Cipher · October 11, 2026
No rule, and no two groups
Gallup’s 463-page volume gives one sentence of classification guidance, on page 2, naming no feature of any letter. The feature description exists only in Riverbank’s Hints to the Decipherer of 1916 — fifteen years later — and names five characteristics, not one. Tested against all five, 149 lower-case italic b show no two-group structure a blind search can find.

Biliteral Cipher · September 9, 2026
His only condition
Bacon’s published system names exactly one requirement — that the concealing text run five times the length of the concealed — and disclaims every other in the same sentence: no other condition or restriction whatsoever is required. No start marker, no stop marker, and nothing to say which text carries a message. What that costs a reader is measured here, against a noise floor.
Biliteral Cipher · September 8, 2026
The whole Folio, one sort
Every lower-case italic w in the thirty-six plays — 3,080 of them by the Bodleian’s own transcription — found on the photographs and measured against both Riverbank stencils. In the fount the plays are set in there is one w, not two, and no third shape either.
Biliteral Cipher · September 7, 2026
Their rules, their page
Riverbank’s own 1916 rule for telling the two forms apart — ten degrees of lean against fifteen — fails on the page it was written for, and every other rule in the booklet fails with it. The w still stands, and a defect in its second-page test is corrected in the open.
Biliteral Cipher · September 5, 2026
Their stencil, our page
Riverbank’s own 1916 photographs, turned into a measuring instrument, sorted every measurable w into exactly the groups they marked — having never seen the page.
